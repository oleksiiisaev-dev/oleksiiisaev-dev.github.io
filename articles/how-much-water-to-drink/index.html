How much water to drink a day: work out your own amount

Vertex One · Updated 2026-11-18

“Drink 8 glasses of water” is easy to remember but ignores your weight, the heat and the food you eat. Here is how much fluid you really need and how not to overdo it.

What the guidelines say

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) considers a total water intake of about 2.0 litres a day for women and 2.5 litres for men adequate for adults. That is all water together: from drinks and from food. About 20–30% comes from food such as vegetables, fruit, soups and porridge.

So from drinks you usually need about 1.5–2 litres for women and 2–2.5 litres for men. Pregnancy adds about 300 ml and breastfeeding 600–700 ml.

Working it out from your weight

A common rough guide is about 30 ml per kilogram of body weight. For someone weighing 70 kg that is about 2.1 litres. It's only a starting point to adjust for how you feel and your conditions.

When you need more

  • Heat and dry air.
  • Exercise: people usually add 400–800 ml per hour of activity, depending on sweat.
  • Fever, vomiting or diarrhoea, where fluid is lost fast — with severe symptoms, see a doctor.

How to tell you're drinking enough

The simplest sign is pale yellow urine and no strong thirst. Dark urine, headache and tiredness can mean you need more fluid.

With heart failure, kidney disease, cirrhosis or some medicines, fluid may need to be limited. Then your doctor sets the amount, and it always takes priority over any general calculation.

Can you drink too much?

Yes, although it's rare. A very large amount of water in a short time (for example several litres within a couple of hours during a long race) can lower blood sodium. Drinking evenly through the day is better than a lot at once.

How to remember to drink

  • A glass of water after waking up and before each meal.
  • A bottle on your desk.
  • A reminder every 2 hours during the day.
